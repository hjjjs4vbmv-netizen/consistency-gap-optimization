\subsection{Stateful trajectory dynamics}
\label{sec:stateful-trajectory-dynamics}

Let $z_k^S$ denote the complete algorithmic state of schedule
$S\in\{A,B\}$ before step $k$, including parameters, optimizer moments, EMA,
and any discrete state that affects the next transition.  Both counterfactual
evaluations consume the same exogenous randomness $\xi_k$.  For a continuous
state block $x_k^S$, suppose the implemented update separates into a declared
linear carryover $C_k x_k^S$ and a newly written increment
$U_{S,k}(z_k^S,\xi_k)$, with the same $C_k$ across the paired evaluations.

Define $\Delta x_k=x_k^B-x_k^A$.  The common-state forcing $b_k^x$ is the
difference between the B and A increments evaluated at $z_k^A$.  The corrected
feedback $\widetilde R_k^x$ is the difference between B increments evaluated
at $z_k^B$ and $z_k^A$.  Adding and subtracting the common-state B increment
gives the exact recursion
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \Delta x_{k+1}
  =b_k^x+C_k\Delta x_k+\widetilde R_k^x.
  }
  \label{eq:carryover-corrected-recursion}
\end{equation}
The three terms distinguish current schedule forcing, mechanical retention of
an existing state difference, and the state dependence of the newly written
update.  This decomposition is algebraic: it requires neither differentiability
of the production transition nor a small trajectory separation.  A mismatch
in optimizer-step execution or another discrete regime violates the shared
$C_k$ premise and must be represented explicitly rather than absorbed into
corrected feedback.

The carryover operator is block-specific.  It is the identity for parameter
retention, the implemented $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$ retentions for executed
RAdam moment updates, and the actual EMA transition for the joint
parameter--EMA block.  Subtracting these known terms prevents ordinary state
memory from being mislabeled as dynamical expansion.  Expansion is a
directional empirical statement about how $\widetilde R_k^x$ changes the
retained separation; it does not follow from a large raw feedback-to-forcing
norm ratio.

The frozen CIFAR transition audit measures non-trivial corrected feedback for
parameters, EMA, and RAdam moments at one audited state.  It establishes that
mechanical carryover and state-dependent incremental feedback can be measured
separately.  The recursion provides an attribution boundary for finite
training: local objective identities are insufficient by themselves to
determine later states or outcomes once forcing, retained history, and
corrected feedback coexist.  Its scope is state-level attribution.  Mapping a
term to FID or to a trajectory-collapse mechanism requires separate,
outcome-aligned evidence.
