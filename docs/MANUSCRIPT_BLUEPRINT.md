# Manuscript Blueprint v1

## Thesis

In fixed-q ECT, pair spacing is an exactly decomposable intervention on the detached target endpoint and explicit denominator weight, but its finite-budget quality consequences emerge through separate, trajectory-dependent learning paths; the complete intervention can advance entry into a high-quality one-step regime without defining a universally best static gap.

## Transition logic

1. Fast generation makes training efficiency relevant; ECT makes pair spacing controllable.
2. Setup reveals that spacing changes two objective components at once.
3. Exact theory separates those components locally but stops at a matched state.
4. The factorial and replay design asks what happens after trajectories separate.
5. Results show early gains, threshold advances, and later heterogeneity.
6. RAdam diagnostics explain why a local scalar identity need not survive state, while the NO-GO blocks causal overreach.
7. The paper closes on the finite-budget control interpretation and the need for cross-q/dataset prospective tests.
